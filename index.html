03 / WHAT'S BEHIND THE NUMBERS

Useful questions.
Visible assumptions.

01

Growth has a mechanism.

More occupied homes add residential demand. Business floor area and industrial utilisation change their own components. Population is not added again as a separate multiplier.

02

Heat has a memory.

Cooling responds to temperature, humidity and a simple thermal lag. Efficiency reduces cooling only. The coefficients are explicit assumptions, not fitted Gulf measurements.

03

Storage has limits.

Dispatch seeks the lowest daily import peak on a discrete charge grid. Power, energy, 90% round-trip efficiency and a 10–90% operating range all apply. The battery starts and ends at 50%.

Model assumptions & limitations

Default district: 12,000 homes, 85% occupied; one reference business estate, a fixed hotel component, and 14 MW of industrial capacity at 70% utilisation. These are illustrative inputs.

Residential demand uses 0.65 kW per occupied home times an activity schedule, plus 0.16 kW per cooling-degree equivalent. Business uses an 8 MW activity component plus cooling; hotels use 4 MW plus cooling. Cooling starts at 24°C, with a humidity uplift above 40% and a 0.65 hourly thermal-memory factor. Initial thermal state equals the first weather hour. Weekends affect the business schedule; Ramadan and holidays are not yet represented.

Solar is a horizontal-array approximation using irradiance, a 0.86 performance factor and a temperature derate. No roof geometry or shading is modelled. Excess solar is curtailed; there is no export. Battery dispatch uses 81 energy states, assumes the full day's scenario is known, and excludes degradation and network constraints. A lower peak does not necessarily mean lower energy use or emissions.

Live mode uses the latest available weather for tomorrow in the selected city's timezone, fetched on request. This release does not preserve a fixed 09:00 forecast vintage. Summer demo weather is generated, not observed. There are no calibrated uncertainty bands or claimed forecast accuracy here.
